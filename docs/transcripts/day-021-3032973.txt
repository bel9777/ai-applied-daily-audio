Alex: Every week, someone on the accounting team ends up drafting the exact same close-status memo from scratch, chasing updates and emailing static spreadsheets around. This is AI Applied, lab 6. Jordan, tell me there is a way to make this draft write itself before we even log in on Monday.
Jordan: There is, and it combines two features in Microsoft 365 Copilot: scheduled prompts and Copilot Pages. Think of a scheduled prompt as a standard prompt with a recurring calendar alert attached. You set your instructions, pick a frequency like weekly at eight in the morning, and Copilot runs a fresh chat in the background under your account. To use it, you need a standard Microsoft 365 Copilot license, and your IT admin must keep optional connected experiences turned on. There is also a hard limit of ten scheduled prompts per person. Copilot Pages is the second half: it takes that chat response and turns it into a live, shared document right next to your chat so people can edit it together instead of passing around email versions.
Alex: So instead of a messy reply-all email thread, we get an automated draft that feeds straight into a shared scratchpad. How do we build this in the lab?
Jordan: In this lab, we use a fictional bicycle company. You do not even need an external file; you paste a small table of departmental budget versus actual figures, plus three open close tasks, directly into your prompt.
Alex: And what are we telling Copilot to do with that data?
Jordan: You tell it to calculate dollar and percentage variances, flag anything where the variance is more than five percent and over five thousand dollars, give short draft commentary on the flagged items, and list the open tasks. You run it once manually to make sure the output looks clean, and then you set it to run on a schedule a few minutes into the future.
Alex: When the timer fires, does an alert blast out to the whole department?
Jordan: Not at all. It shows up quietly as a new conversation in your personal chat list. Treat it like a junior preparer dropping a rough draft on your desk. Once you see the output, you select the option to edit it in Pages. That opens a side-by-side canvas where you can ask Copilot to add things like a reviewer sign-off block with lines for dates and signatures.
Alex: Let us talk about where this breaks. Language models are not famous for spreadsheet-level arithmetic or knowing when to stay quiet.
Jordan: Exactly, which is why a scheduled prompt is not an internal accounting control. You have to check the math yourself. In our sample data, travel expenses were five thousand dollars under budget, which is a twenty-eight percent variance. Models frequently skip favorable variances because they assume a variance only matters when you overspend.
Alex: And I am guessing it tries to invent reasons for why an overspend happened too.
Jordan: Almost every time. If your prompt does not state why marketing or IT went over budget, Copilot might hallucinate an ad campaign or software upgrade. If the source data does not explain it, you have to wipe out that commentary. The audit evidence is your human sign-off on the Page, never the raw automated chat.
